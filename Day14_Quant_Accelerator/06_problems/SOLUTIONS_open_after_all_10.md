# Solutions
1. One huge outlier inflates the std and hides itself and its neighbours (masking). The MAD has a 50% breakdown point. ×1.4826 makes it comparable to σ under normality. *Use estimators the errors can't corrupt.*
2. A one-day bad print: a jump followed by an equal reversal. Proof: NSE's bhavcopy for 2010-02-05 shows a close of 476.85 (÷10 = 47.685) vs Yahoo's 54.5, i.e. 14.3% off. *A spike that reverses next day is guilty until the official file says otherwise.*
3. 7/30 ≈ 23%. Wilson 95% interval ≈ 12%–41%. Huge. Since your signal uses the index, which reconciled perfectly (D3), the signal is OK. But your execution/return series (NIFTYBEES) isn't. Rebuild it from bhavcopy. *Audit each data role separately: signal, execution, benchmark.*
4. Vendor-filled placeholder bars (a quote carried forward, no trades). A backtest "fills" at prices nobody traded, which understates vol and fakes fills. *A price with zero volume is an opinion, not a trade.*
5. Real: it's NSE's own record. Equity ETFs have no pre-open call auction (per NSE/CMTR/76101, from 7 Sep 2026 their book types are RL and SL only), so the open is just the first continuous trade. A thin first print 6.2% above the previous close is still inside the price band. Your model must NOT assume an open fill: use limit orders (the Day 7 rules) and model the fill on a protected price. *"Real" and "tradeable for you" are different questions.*
6. You silently lose every year before the rename, then the series appears to "start" in 2015 or so. If you then forward-fill or join, you get gaps or a misaligned history. *Keep a mapping table: names, symbols and ISINs, each with validity dates.*
7. Back-filling the daily rows with the NEXT month-end's signal puts information from the end of the month into every earlier day of that month. *Fill forward, never backward, in time.*
8. The leak reaches up to ~21 trading days ahead, so a 1-day delay still leaks. The perturbation test catches any read past t, whatever the horizon. *Test the information boundary directly. Don't infer it from performance.*
9. Not zero. (a) NIFTY 50's own history is a survivor of index reconstitutions. Fine for an index strategy, but don't compare it with stock picks. (b) Picking "the ETF that exists and is liquid today" (NIFTYBEES) is a survivor choice. Others were closed or merged. (c) Any pre-ETF period backfilled from the index. *Ask what died that you never saw.*
10. (a) Pre-reg not verifiable or not publicly timestamped, or rules changed without a v2. (b) Data FAILs with no written decision, or the holdout was opened or touched. Also: a signal function that fails the perturbation test. *A gate graded on P&L isn't a gate.*
