# Day 10: 10 problems (do before reading the solutions)
1. Hou, Xue & Zhang tried to replicate 452 anomalies. Roughly what share failed |t| > 1.96, and what share failed the multiple-test hurdle (2.78)? Which category failed most, and why does that matter for a cost-sensitive retail trader?
2. Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen say most factors DO replicate. Reconcile them with Hou et al. in 4 lines. (Hint: what each counts as success, Bayesian shrinkage, the 13 themes.)
3. Novy-Marx & Velikov: what turnover level roughly separates anomalies that survive costs from those that don't? Translate that into trades per year for a single-ETF rule.
4. The overnight NIFTYBEES card: gross 9.1 bps per day, cost 13.9 bps per round trip. Even with NO haircut, what's the yearly result at 245 trades? What's the lesson about "statistically significant"?
5. Turn-of-month: gross 52 bps per trade. With a 42% keep and 13.9 bps cost, what's the net per trade? Why is "net < 2x cost" a sensible PARK rule?
6. A card with net Sharpe 0.3: how many years are needed for t = 3? With net Sharpe 0.8? What does this say about discovering slow strategies?
7. Explain "confirm mode" vs "discovery mode". Why is confirm mode NOT a loophole? What must the literature evidence satisfy?
8. Which AHM protocol category did you personally break most in Days 4–8? Be honest; cite a trial.
9. Pre-mortem: write the single most likely failure story for the 12-month trend card in 3 sentences.
10. Your mentor kills a card you love. List three things that are NOT valid defences.
