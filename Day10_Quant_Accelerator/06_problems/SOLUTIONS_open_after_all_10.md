# Solutions
1. About **65%** failed |t| ≥ 1.96, and **82.1%** failed the 2.78 multiple-test hurdle (Hou–Xue–Zhang, RFS 2020 version). **96% of the trading-frictions category** failed. Those are the liquidity and microstructure "edges" a retail trader is most tempted by, and they mostly lived in microcaps. *Assume a published edge is weaker than printed.*
2. Hou et al. use strict frequentist tests on value-weighted portfolios with NYSE breakpoints. JKP use a Bayesian model that pools related factors into 13 themes, shrinks noisy estimates and tests out of sample across 93 countries. Both agree magnitudes are smaller than first claimed; they disagree on whether "smaller" means "zero". *For us: themes like momentum and value are real; single clever signals usually aren't.*
3. One-sided monthly turnover **below about 50%** mostly survives costs; few higher-turnover strategies do. For one ETF in or out, that's at most about 6 round trips a year (12 one-way switches). *Slow wins.*
4. (9.1 − 13.9) × 245 ≈ **−11.8% a year** before any haircut. A t of 2.49 on gross returns says nothing about net returns. *Significant ≠ tradable.*
5. 52 × 0.42 − 13.9 ≈ **8.0 bps**. One extra bps of slippage per side, a wider spread on month-end days, or a slightly lower true edge erases most of it. *Demand a margin of safety bigger than your cost estimate's error (Day 7: calculator within 20%).*
6. Years = (3/SR)²: SR 0.3 → **100 years**; SR 0.8 → **about 14 years**. *Slow, low-Sharpe strategies can't be discovered in one market's history; they can only be confirmed against strong outside evidence.*
7. Discovery: our data alone must show t > 3. Confirm: the edge is already established elsewhere at t ≥ 3, with a payer and a post-publication record; our data only needs to agree in sign and size, with the test pre-registered. It's not a loophole because the literature must be out-of-sample evidence (other markets or centuries, post-publication), and we still haircut and test once. *You're borrowing evidence, so borrow only from strong lenders.*
8. Personal; typical honest answers: multiple testing (running extra variants without logging) or data (peeking at post-2018 charts). *Naming it is the fix.*
9. Example: "2027 was choppy. NIFTY crossed its 12-month average five times, each exit and re-entry cost 2–4% plus short-term tax, and the filter lagged the rebound. I stopped it in month 5, just before the trend it was built for." *Whipsaw and abandonment are the main killers of trend rules.*
10. "It made money in a backtest I ran on 2019+", "I've spent a lot of time on it", "a YouTuber or famous trader uses it." *Sunk cost and authority are not evidence.*
