# Solutions
1. A few big trending spells (2009, 2014, 2017) carry it; the typical spell is roughly flat after whipsaws. Point 2 passes on average (about 54x cost), but the edge is lumpy. *Expect long boring or losing stretches; that's where people quit.*
2. Part-time beta: about 28–40% market exposure on average, no evidence of skill (t well below 2). *It's a risk-reduction tool, not an alpha engine.*
3. It cut max drawdown from about −45% to −25% (daily data) while keeping most of the return, which helps you survive and stick with the plan. It's useless if a simple 50/50 NIFTY+cash or NIFTY+gold mix does the same more cheaply. *Always compare to the dumb alternative (Day 9: the 50/50 hold beat the filter).*
4. (a) Noise: with only 10–20 switches, one unlucky day after a switch moves CAGR a lot. (b) Rule shape: the 12-month signal flips right at sharp reversals, so a day's delay hurts more. Live, assume the delay. *Never backtest at a price you couldn't trade.*
5. (a) The signal uses the 3:30 pm close, but your order would have had to go in before 3:00–3:30 pm. (b) After 2026 the close is set by the Closing Auction Session for CAS stocks. Assuming you trade at the close then needs a CAS order, which has its own price band and fill risk. *Trade next day, or model the auction honestly.*
6. About ₹3 lakh a day. You trade ₹1 lakh about twice a year, so you're a rounding error. A ₹500 cr fund would need weeks. (NIFTYBEES is far more liquid now; check the current figure, but that's a liquidity fact, not return data, so looking is allowed.) *Capacity limits big players, which is partly why small players can harvest slow, low-capacity edges.*
7. The ETFs started trading in 2022 and later. The index's earlier history was back-calculated with today's rules, by people who knew the rules worked. That's backfill/selection bias. *Use the IIMA WML factor (built ex-ante) as the evidence, then forward test.*
8. In "panic" states, after market falls with high volatility, when the market rebounds sharply and past losers surge. A long-only ETF has no short-loser leg, so the worst crash channel is smaller, but it still lags in sharp junk rallies and has high beta in crashes. *Long-only momentum is market + a tilt; score its beta.*

The evidence comes from 140 years across dozens of markets, and our data only has to agree, which it roughly does.
