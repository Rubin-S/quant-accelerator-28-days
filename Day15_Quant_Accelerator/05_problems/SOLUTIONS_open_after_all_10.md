# Solutions
1. To know today is the last session, you need to know tomorrow is in a new month (or a holiday). That's future information, plus the NSE holiday calendar as it stood back then. Deciding on the first session of the new month, using the previous close, is the honest version: one day later. *If a rule needs tomorrow's date, it's a look-ahead rule.*
2. Realistic: you'd place the order near t+1's close using live prices, and units come from a price you can see. Optimistic: you're assuming a fill AT the close (no auction risk, no partials). Slippage partly covers that. Day 26 measures the real gap. *Name the assumption and price it.*
3. The decision at close t−2 fills at close t−1 and is then held through day t's return. One shift for "decided → filled", one for "filled → earning". *Count the steps in time, not the lines of code.*
4. Leftover cash from integer rounding, plus 6% interest, grows past `min_trade_value` (₹1,000) and gets topped up into the ETF. It's harmless, but it's a trade you didn't intend: raise `min_trade_value` or add a band. *Read your trade log, not just the summary.*
5. EWMA vol moves a little every day, so the target weight moves every day, and any change above ₹1,000 trades. A band ignores re-sizes below 10 percentage points. Entries and exits always go through. Costs drop from ₹13.6k to ₹5.3k. *Sizing precision you pay for daily is usually noise.*
6. (a) Faber is already a volatility overlay: it exits in the high-vol drawdowns, so vol scaling double-counts. (b) The sample is one market and about 11 years: wide error bars, so a 0.1 Sharpe difference means little. Also, the cap at 1.0 means it can only scale down, never up, so it gives up return in calm bull runs. *A published effect on 60 assets isn't a promise for your one asset with your constraints.*
7. Costs and slippage took a little. Mostly, the 6% hurdle: excess Sharpe subtracts cash. The 0%-rf version credits the strategy for nothing. *Always compare against what idle money earns.*
8. Test 9 (flat earns cash) wouldn't catch it. Test 7 (cash ledger) might not either. A targeted test: on the day of a big buy, interest must be accrued on the pre-fill cash balance. Assert the interest that day equals cash_before_fill × (growth − 1). *Every ordering decision deserves a test that pins it.*
