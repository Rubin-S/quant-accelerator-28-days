# Solutions
1. A 10m filter can't trade until it has 10 month-ends, so from Sep 2007 it sat in cash by construction while buy & hold took the full GFC hit. Report the aligned +18 pp, and show the +34 pp once as a warning. *Compare from the first day both could act.*
2. You can claim nothing about Sharpe: both bands straddle zero and overlap almost entirely. 11 years of daily data can't separate 0.18 from 0.19. *No alpha claim; that was the pre-reg's point.*
3. Volatility is estimated far more precisely than the mean: its SE shrinks with the number of observations, while the mean's precision depends on calendar time. Exposure ~70% mechanically cuts vol. *Measure what the data can measure.*
4. "8m was chosen after seeing it; 10m was chosen from Faber 2007 before any data. The 8m vs 10m gap is far inside the noise (problem 2). Switching = new pre-reg + trial count up + DSR haircut, and I'd still expect the gap to vanish OOS." *The grid is for stability, not for shopping.*
5. The three lengths are almost one bet (corr ~0.9+). Use the total trial-log count for the family as N (conservative), and report effective N beside it; never use 1.15 alone, because that hides the other ideas you tried. *When unsure, pick the N that hurts you.*
6. Shallower drawdowns that take longer to recover: whipsaws, plus sitting in cash during rebounds (2009, 2012) delays the new high. As the human, 3.5 years without a new high is when people quit or override. Put it in the Day 21 rulebook. *The pain you'll feel is time, not depth.*
7. A strategy object holds state (`w`, `prev`). The second run started already "in", so the frictionless rerun traded differently from the costed one, and the difference was path, not cost. Guarded by `test_fresh_strategy_objects_matter`; the runs now use factories. *Reused state is look-ahead's cousin.*
8. A 1 pp/yr error on the cash leg × 30% time in cash ≈ 0.3 pp CAGR. Excess Sharpe moves by roughly the full 1 pp / vol (14%) ≈ 0.07, because the benchmark subtraction applies every day. *A sloppy cash leg moves Sharpe more than CAGR.*
9. Longer blocks are safer for Sharpe bands because they keep regime clustering (months flat, months in). The automatic rule reads short-lag autocorrelation, and a strategy's flat stretches look like zero-variance noise to it. Check sensitivity at 5/20/60. *Pick the block length that respects the strategy's rhythm.*
10. Written predictions turn Day 20 into a test of your understanding, not just of the strategy, and stop you from rationalising after you see it. *Predict, then look.*
