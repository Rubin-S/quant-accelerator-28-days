# Solutions
1. Cost drag ≈ 11 × 14 bps / 10 yrs ≈ 1.5 bps a year, so ×3 costs ~5 bps a year. Tiny. The overlay's excess return over buy & hold is already ≈ 0 (Sharpe diff −0.01), so there's no cost cushion to measure. Costs don't matter here; the edge was never in returns. *Low turnover makes costs irrelevant, and it doesn't create an edge.*
2. Not good news, just noise: results bounce with the decision day, so "first session" isn't special. If a later fill helped by design, you'd want a reason; there isn't one. *A stress that helps is still telling you the noise level.*
3. Split capital into, say, 4 tranches deciding on sessions 1, 6, 11, 16; each holds ¼. Timing luck shrinks ~1/N (their result). Cost: 4× the trade tickets (minimum-charge drag at small size) and more complex plumbing. *Diversify when, not just what.*
4. "All of the overlay's return advantage rests on one round trip out of 11; we make no return claim." *If one trade carries it, it's an anecdote, not an edge.*
5. Replacing a trade with cash lowers vol and DD mechanically, so those columns would show fake comfort. A fair test would also drop buy & hold's best days (or the same dates) so both sides lose the same luck. *Stress both sides equally.*
6. Being out of the market ~30% of the time alone, at random times, gives ~0.87; your rule gets 0.69. About 40% of the cut is exposure; the rest is timing, and only the timing part is "skill", at borderline significance. *Separate what cash does from what the signal does.*
7. The DD edge isn't clearly better than random timing at the same exposure, and it depends on whether Oct 2008 is in the window. It's a fragile claim: keep it as secondary at most. *Two weak signals pointing the same way still don't make a strong one.*
8. Vol comes from the equity leg; cash adds almost no variance. Sharpe is measured over cash, so changing the cash rate changes the numerator for both sides, and more for the side holding more cash. *Your benchmark rate is part of your result.*
9. Vol: "In about 99 of 100 resampled histories, the overlay ran with clearly lower volatility." DD: "In about 4 of 5 it had a shallower worst drawdown. That's encouraging, but it also depends on a single crash." Never "99% chance it works". *Probability statements describe resampled pasts, not the future.*
